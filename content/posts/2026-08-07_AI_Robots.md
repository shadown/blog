## What Changed: July 16 → Aug 7, 2026

### 1. US Bans Foreign-Made Humanoids, Robot Dogs, and Solar Inverters (Jul 29) — ⚠️ The Defining Event

The Trump administration, via the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), **banned the import of new foreign-made humanoid robots, robot dogs, and power inverters**, citing an "unacceptable" threat to national security. The move is a **Department of Defense-driven expansion** of the FCC's "Covered List," originally published in 2021, which identifies communications equipment and services posing a national-security threat.

Key mechanics (per TechCrunch, AP, and IEEE Spectrum analysis):

- **Scope:** Applies to "advanced robotic devices" — mobile systems with on-board sensing and communications (drones are already covered/exempt). Robot dogs (quadrupeds) and humanoid robots are the visible targets; power inverters are included because of grid connectivity.
- **Affected supply chain:** Largely hits **China**, which had the vast majority of the global market share. Roughly **15,000 humanoid robots shipped worldwide in 2025**, with China's two largest makers (Unitree, UBTech) accounting for the majority.
- **Exceptions:** Available if the government finds a device does not pose a national-security risk; applicants must submit to review.
- **Existing devices unaffected** — the ban applies to new imports.
- **Rationale cited:** Foreign devices could be remotely controlled, used for surveillance, or leveraged in cyberattacks. The DOD specifically cited IEEE Spectrum's reporting on a **critical vulnerability in Unitree robots**.
- **Industry reaction (IEEE Spectrum, Aug 4):** US companies largely supportive but warn that **China also produces a large fraction of robot components**, even for robots assembled in the US, giving Beijing leverage. Ghost Robotics CEO Gavin Kenneally was explicit about "active and purposeful spyware" risk from Chinese robot strategy.
- **China retaliation:** Beijing rebuffed the ban, with a Foreign Ministry spokesperson saying China will use "**all measures necessary**" to protect its businesses (CNBC, Jul 29; Supply Chain Brain, Aug 3). Xi Jinping is expected to meet Trump in Washington in September.

**Why it matters:** This is the first hard US import restriction on humanoids. It converts the "national industrial strategy" thesis into active **trade policy**, validates earlier predictions about export controls, and forces Western humanoid makers to prove domestic supply-chain resilience. It also creates a two-track market: Chinese-built robots for non-US markets, US/Western-built robots for US customers.

### 2. Unitree Prices IPO at ~$9 Billion (Aug 6)

**Unitree** priced its IPO at a ~**$9 billion valuation** (CNBC, Aug 6), following its June 18 listing announcement — the first major humanoid robot public listing. IPO subscription opened Aug 5; the company is profitable, contrasting with higher-valuation, no-revenue peers. This cements the humanoid public-markets category and gives China a flagship listed champion just as US import restrictions land.

### 3. BYD Confirms Its First Humanoid Robot Will Debut in August (Jul 27)

**BYD** — the world's largest EV maker — confirmed plans to unveil its **first humanoid robot in August** (CnEVPost, Gasgoo, Yahoo Finance). BYD becomes the second Chinese automaker (after XPeng) to enter humanoids directly, and signals that Chinese humanoid supply will be dominated by vertically integrated auto-scale manufacturers. "Tesla's Optimus May Soon Have Company."

### 4. Agility Robotics Plants Its Flag in Tesla's Backyard (Jul 17)

Agility opened a **60,000-square-foot training facility in Fremont, California** — just up the highway from Tesla's Optimus factory. Key facts:

- **Digit is already revenue-generating**: carrying totes/bins for Amazon, GXO, Schaeffler, and Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada; **$300M in contract orders** secured; 100,000+ totes moved at GXO.
- **30+ customers** in talks for deployment.
- **Digit v5** (expected this fall) will sense humans and won't require robot-only zones.
- CEO Peggy Johnson and chairman Damion Shelton emphasize a **practical autonomy approach**: safety stack kept off generative AI, generative AI used for application scaling.
- Reverse-merger path to become the **first pure-play US humanoid public company** remains on track for later this year.

The Fremont facility is explicitly a skills-learning center — training Digit in environments that mirror customer sites — not a factory. It's a signal that Agility's bottleneck is deployment capability, not capital.

### 5. Walden Robotics Exits Stealth — $300M at $1.1B Valuation, TRI Spinout (Jul 15-16)

**Walden Robotics**, a spinout of Toyota Research Institute (TRI), emerged from stealth with **$300M in funding at a $1.1B valuation** (IEEE Spectrum, Aug 3). Founded/led by Russ Tedrake, one of the most respected names in robot control. Differentiators:

- **Wheeled humanoid, not bipedal** — explicitly rejects the "legs for everything" hype; a large wheeled base carries more batteries and payload for manufacturing work.
- **Application-first**: developed commercial use cases before the robot, rather than robot-first-then-market.
- **Toyota partnership** — given TRI origins, a deep Toyota relationship is expected.
- High-payload industrial/manufacturing focus; confidentiality around current commercial partners.

Walden is the strongest signal yet that the industry is bifurcating: **bipedal generalists (Figure, 1X, Unitree) vs. pragmatic task-focused platforms (Walden, Agility, Gritt)**.

### 6. "Humanoid" (UK) Raises $152M — Europe's First Pure-Play Humanoid Unicorn (Jul 21)

UK-based **Humanoid** raised **$152M at a $1.35B post-money valuation**, becoming **Europe's first pure-play humanoid robotics unicorn** and the UK's first AI-robotics unicorn (Business Wire, FT, Forbes). FT framed it as a proof point for European tech scaling. Europe now has its own scaled pure-play, adding to the public-markets roster.

### 7. Enigma Raises $71M Seed — Human-Robot Interaction Lab (Jul 27)

**Enigma** (Israel/California), founded by former Unit 8200 cyber researchers Jonathan Jacobi (Microsoft's youngest-ever employee) and Gal Niv, raised a **$71M seed** led by Index Ventures and Ribbit Capital, with Sarah Guo (Conviction). Their thesis: the interface is the bottleneck. They're launching a **large-scale public experiment with 100+ proprietary AI robots** (paintbrush drawing, sword-fighting, chemistry flasks) in hangars in Israel and California, to learn how humans naturally want to control robots — text, audio, video demonstration, or direct manipulation. Partners in healthcare, logistics, entertainment. A bet that **HRI (human-robot interaction) data is the next unlock** — consistent with the Encord/Zander brain-wave story.

### 8. Gritt Exits Stealth — $32M for Robots That Build Solar Plants (Jul 21)

**Gritt** (CMU roboticists Puneet Puri and Vishal Dugar) exited stealth with a **$26M Series A led by Obvious Ventures** (USV, Active Impact participating), ~$32M total. Instead of building humanoid hardware, Gritt's AI controls **off-the-shelf skidders and robotic arms (Kawasaki)** to handle solar panels with sub-millimeter accuracy. Results:

- Crews install **3,000-4,000 panels/day with Gritt vs. ~800 without**.
- **2.8 GW of solar contracted** over the next 18 months; customers include three of the top-10 US power-construction companies.
- Targeting **48 systems deployed within six months**.

Gritt is the clearest validation that **non-humanoid, task-specific physical AI is already economic** — the "AI on industrial robots" wave that often gets overshadowed by humanoid headlines.

## Core Thesis: From "National Industrial Strategy" to "Trade War Phase"

**July (National Industrial Strategy):** The prior thesis was that humanoids had crossed from commercial viability into a government-backed industrial strategy phase — Japan's FRONTia, Korea's $1T plan, China's 85% production share, US laissez-faire.

**August (Trade War Phase):** The July 29 FCC ban changes the regime. Humanoids are no longer just an industrial-strategy prize; they are now **items on the US-China export/import control list**, alongside routers, Hikvision/Dahua cameras, and solar inverters. Implications:

1. **Two supply chains form:** Chinese-built humanoids serve China + non-US markets; US/Western-built serve the US and allied markets. Component leverage (China makes a large fraction of robot parts) will shape who can actually scale.
2. **Export controls were predicted; import bans arrived first.** The earlier prediction of "Chinese humanoid export controls within 18 months" is now flanked by US import restrictions — the direction flipped faster than expected.
3. **The US pure-play winners get a moat:** Agility, Figure, 1X (Norwegian-US), and Boston Dynamics/Hyundai now face reduced Chinese competition inside the US — a tailwind for the Agility SPAC.
4. **China's response will define the next 6 months:** retaliation threats + Xi-Trump September meeting + BYD's August debut + Unitree's ~$9B IPO give Beijing multiple levers.
5. **The economics are still data-bound:** Encord's "5x YouTube's video corpus" framing and Enigma's interface experiment both point at the real bottleneck: physical training data and interaction data — not hardware.
